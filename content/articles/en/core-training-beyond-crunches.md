When people think "core training," crunches usually come to mind first. But the abdominal muscles do far more than bend the spine forward. A large part of their job is resisting movement that shouldn't happen, which is a very different skill from repeatedly flexing the trunk.

## The core's real job: resisting, not just moving

In everyday life and in training, the core frequently works isometrically — bracing to keep the spine stable while the arms, legs, or an external load try to move it. Carrying a heavy bag on one side, pressing overhead, or bracing during a squat all demand this kind of stability rather than active bending.

## Anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral-flexion

These three terms describe the core's stabilizing roles. Anti-extension resists the lower back arching excessively, as challenged by planks or rollouts. Anti-rotation resists twisting forces, as in a Pallof press. Anti-lateral-flexion resists side-bending, as trained by a suitcase carry. Together, these cover far more real-world demand than spinal flexion alone.

## Practical exercises that reflect this

A front plank trains anti-extension; a side plank or suitcase carry trains anti-lateral-flexion; a Pallof press or an anti-rotation hold trains resistance to twisting. None of these require crunching the spine repeatedly, and most people find they carry over more directly to lifting, carrying, and daily movement than crunches alone.

## A practical next step

Try adding one stability-focused core exercise to your next session — a 20-30 second plank hold, a short suitcase carry, or a few slow Pallof press reps per side. Notice how bracing against resistance feels different from simply curling forward, and build your core work around a mix of both over time.
