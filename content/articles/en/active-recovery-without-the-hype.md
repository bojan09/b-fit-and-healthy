Active recovery is simply low-intensity movement done on a day between harder sessions. It is easy to oversell, so it helps to be clear about what it actually offers.

## What active recovery really is

A brisk walk, an easy bike ride, gentle mobility work, or a relaxed swim all count. The goal is to keep blood moving and joints comfortable without adding meaningful fatigue. It is not a second workout in disguise, and effort should stay noticeably below your training sessions — you should be able to hold a conversation the entire time without strain.

## What it can and cannot do

Light movement can make you feel less stiff and can support a more normal mood and appetite after a demanding session. It does not "flush out toxins" or clear lactate faster than simply resting does — that idea is a popular myth, not a documented mechanism, and lactate is cleared within an hour or two of any session regardless of what you do afterward. Active recovery will not undo a poor sleep pattern, a string of high-intensity sessions, or an underlying injury; it is a mild supplement to rest, not a substitute for it, and it will not speed up how quickly damaged tissue repairs itself.

## Choosing the right dose

Keep active recovery short and comfortable, generally 20 to 40 minutes at a pace where conversation stays easy. If you finish more tired than when you started, or if your legs feel heavier the next day, the session was too hard to count as recovery. Some weeks a slow walk is enough; other weeks, especially after a demanding training block or a stretch of poor sleep, full rest may serve you better than any movement at all. There is no fixed rule for which one to choose — it depends on how the rest of the week has gone.

## Try it this week

After your next hard session, schedule one easy 20-minute walk instead of a full rest day or another workout. Notice how it feels compared to sitting still all day — then decide, without pressure, whether to repeat it next week. If it starts to feel like an obligation rather than something mild and pleasant, that is a sign to scale it back or skip it.
