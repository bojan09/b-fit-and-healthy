Online routines often list ten steps: dim lights, journal, stretch, tea, specific breathing counts, and more. Most people try this for a few nights and quit, not because sleep hygiene doesn't matter, but because the routine itself became another demanding task at the end of a long day. A routine that adds pressure defeats its own purpose.

## Fewer elements, done consistently

A realistic routine needs only two or three anchors: a rough bedtime window, dimmer lighting in the last hour, and one calming activity you actually enjoy, like reading or light stretching. Consistency matters more than variety. A short routine you repeat nightly trains your body to recognize the wind-down signal far better than an elaborate one you skip half the time. If a step feels like a chore rather than a comfort, it's usually the first one worth cutting.

## Light and screens matter more than the activity itself

Bright light, especially from screens held close to the face, can delay the body's natural signal to feel sleepy. You don't need to ban screens entirely — lowering brightness, switching to non-stimulating content, and stepping away from the phone thirty minutes before bed usually does more good than any single relaxation technique. Dimming overhead lights in the rest of the room in that same window reinforces the same signal without requiring any extra activity at all.

## A rough bedtime beats a perfect one

Going to bed within roughly the same hour-wide window most nights supports a more predictable sleep-wake rhythm than chasing an exact bedtime you rarely hit. Weekend flexibility is fine; the goal is a general pattern, not a strict schedule that adds pressure and makes sleep harder to reach when you inevitably miss it. Treating an occasional late night as a normal variation, rather than a failure, keeps the routine sustainable over months rather than days.

## What to do tonight

Pick one screen-free, calming activity you'll actually repeat — not the most impressive one. Do it for ten to fifteen minutes before your rough bedtime tonight, and again tomorrow. A simple routine you keep beats an elaborate one you abandon by Thursday.
