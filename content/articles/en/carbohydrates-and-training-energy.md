Carbohydrates break down into glucose, which muscles and the brain use as a fast, accessible fuel source. For most training — from a brisk walk to a hard interval session — carbohydrate is the body's preferred energy source, especially as intensity rises.

## Why the body reaches for carbs first

Muscles store glucose as glycogen, a form that can be released quickly during effort. Fat is also a fuel, but it releases energy more slowly, which matters less at rest and more once effort intensifies. This is why sessions that feel flat or unusually heavy sometimes trace back to low carbohydrate intake in the days before, not a lack of fitness.

## Timing around workouts, kept simple

A meal containing carbohydrate a few hours before training helps top up glycogen and supports steadier energy through the session. After training, carbohydrate alongside protein helps refill glycogen stores and supports recovery. None of this needs to be precise — a normal meal at a normal time covers most people's needs without special products or narrow windows.

## The "low-carb for everyone" myth

Very low carbohydrate approaches can work for specific goals or medical situations, but they are not a universal requirement for health or fitness. Reducing carbohydrate sharply often reduces training capacity and can make hard sessions feel disproportionately difficult. For most people training regularly, carbohydrate is a tool that supports performance rather than something to minimize by default.

## A useful next step

Notice a training session where energy felt unusually low. Check what you ate that day, particularly earlier meals. Add a straightforward carbohydrate source — rice, oats, potatoes, fruit, or whole grain bread — to a nearby meal and observe whether the next similar session feels different.
