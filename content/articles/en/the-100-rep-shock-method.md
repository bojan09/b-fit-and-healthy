This is a simple, brutal format: for a given exercise, chase 100 total repetitions using whatever weight lets you get meaningfully close to that number without turning the last sets into a different, sloppier exercise. It's built for a muscle group that's stopped responding to your normal rep ranges, not for everyday training.

## How it works

Pick a weight and, after a proper warm-up, perform as many reps as you can with genuinely good technique — say 25. You now have 75 left. Rest briefly, then go again — maybe you get 20 this time. Keep repeating short, hard sets like this, resting only as long as you need to keep the reps honest, until your running total reaches 100. The number of sets it takes doesn't matter; only the total rep count does. Use 3–5 exercises per muscle group in a session, applying this method to each.

## Why it can help

Training the same rep range week after week is one of the most common reasons a lift stops progressing — the muscle adapts to a narrow stimulus and simply stops being challenged by it. A short block of much higher total volume at a lighter load is a different enough stimulus to sometimes restart progress, and it's a format lifters have used informally for decades as an occasional plateau-breaker rather than a everyday programming choice.

## Our take: use this as a rare finisher, not a routine

Chasing 100 reps this way accumulates a large amount of fatigue very quickly, and technique quality tends to slip as the total climbs — which is exactly the tradeoff you're making on purpose here, so it needs guardrails. Reserve it for a muscle group you already train regularly and know well technically, not a movement you're still learning. Once form clearly breaks down for a handful of reps in a row, stop that exercise rather than grinding to hit exactly 100 — the number is a target, not a rule that overrides your joints. Because the fatigue and soreness from this style run high, don't repeat it on the same muscle group more than roughly once every couple of weeks, and don't stack it in the same session as your normal heavy work for that muscle — use it as the whole session, or as a rare finisher on a lighter day.
