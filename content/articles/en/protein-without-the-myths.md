Protein supplies amino acids used to repair tissue after training. It also supports enzymes, hormones, skin, and immune function. It is useful food—not a specialist shortcut.

## Start with the daily total

For many healthy, active adults, **1.4–1.8 grams per kilogram of bodyweight** is a practical range. A 70 kg person would aim for roughly 100–125 g per day. Treat that number as a working range, not a pass-or-fail score.

## Build ordinary meals

Spread the target across three or four meals. Eggs, yoghurt, beans, lentils, tofu, fish, poultry, and lean meat can all contribute. A familiar protein source at each meal is usually enough to begin.

## Timing is secondary

The hour after training is not a narrow emergency window. A normal meal within a few hours works for most people. Consistent training, adequate food, and enough sleep matter more than perfect timing.

## A useful next step

Track three normal days before changing anything. Look for the meal that regularly lacks protein, then improve that one meal first.
