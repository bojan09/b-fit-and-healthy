Training creates a reason for the body to adapt. Sleep provides time for many recovery processes and helps you return with better attention, coordination, and effort.

## Recovery is more than soreness

Short sleep can affect mood, appetite, decision-making, and perceived effort. You may still complete a workout, but technique and load choices can become less reliable.

## Protect a repeatable schedule

Keep wake time reasonably consistent, prepare the room, and give yourself a predictable wind-down. A routine you can repeat is more valuable than ten rules you abandon after two nights.

## Adjust training when needed

One poor night does not require panic. Use a longer warm-up and keep difficult sets controlled. Persistent sleep problems or severe daytime sleepiness deserve professional assessment.

## A better minimum routine

For two weeks, keep the same wake time and put the phone away 30 minutes before bed. Review what actually changed before adding another habit.
