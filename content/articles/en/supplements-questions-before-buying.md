Supplement aisles and ads promise a lot, and it can be hard to tell what is worth considering. Rather than focusing on any specific product, it helps to have a short list of questions to work through first. This is general education, not a recommendation to take or avoid any particular supplement.

## Is there an actual gap it's filling?

Most nutrients are best obtained through food, and supplementation tends to matter most when there is a specific, identified gap, such as a nutrient that is hard to get enough of from diet alone, a documented deficiency, or a life stage with distinct needs. Starting from "what am I actually short on" is more useful than starting from "what might help."

## Is it third-party tested?

Supplement regulation varies by country, and manufacturing quality can vary widely between brands. Independent testing certifications indicate that a product has been checked for accurate labeling and absence of contaminants, which matters more for supplements than for many other consumer products since they aren't always reviewed before reaching shelves.

## Are the claims realistic?

Claims that a product will dramatically transform body composition, energy, or performance on its own are a signal to slow down. Most supplements, where evidence supports them at all, offer a modest effect on top of consistent training, sleep, and eating patterns, not a replacement for them.

## What to check before you buy

Before purchasing, ask whether you can identify the specific gap you are addressing, whether the product carries independent testing, and whether it might interact with any medication you take, which is worth confirming with a pharmacist or doctor rather than guessing. If you're unsure whether you need something at all, that question itself is worth bringing to a registered dietitian or your doctor before spending money on it.
