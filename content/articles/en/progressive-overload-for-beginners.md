The body adapts when a familiar movement becomes gradually more demanding. More weight is one option, but it is not the only form of progress.

## Progress has several forms

You can complete another controlled repetition, improve range of motion, use cleaner technique, shorten unnecessary pauses, or add a small amount of load. Choose one change at a time.

## Keep useful records

Log the exercise, load, repetitions, and a short note about difficulty. Without a record, progress becomes a feeling instead of data.

## Earn the next increase

When every planned set reaches the top of its repetition range with stable technique, make the smallest practical increase. A slower progression that preserves form is still progression.

## Expect uneven weeks

Sleep, stress, and schedule affect performance. Judge the trend across several sessions instead of forcing a record every workout.
